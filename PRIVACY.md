# Privacy notice

ScrapJob collects personal data about people who did not ask to be in it. This notice says what it collects, why, and
how anyone in it can object. It covers one copy of the tool, run by one person for their own job search. The same text
is shown in the app at `/privacy`.

*This is not legal advice. If you share the tool with other people, each of them becomes a controller of the data they
collect and needs their own notice.*

## Who is responsible

The person running this copy of ScrapJob is the **controller** (GDPR Art. 4(7)). Fill in your details before you
contact anyone:

> Controller: **[your name]**, contact: **[your email address]**

## Purpose

To find people to contact about a job opening: recruiters, HR staff, hiring managers, team leads and founders at
employers in the area the user searches. The data is not used for marketing, not sold and not shared.

## Lawful basis

Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)): the user's interest in finding work, and the contact's professional role in hiring.
The balancing test is written down in [`docs/LIA.md`](docs/LIA.md).

## Categories of data

Professional identity only:

- name, job title and department;
- employer and work location (city, country);
- a work email address or phone number, only when the person or their employer published that exact value;
- a public professional profile URL;
- for each of these, the URL of the page it was seen on, a short excerpt of that page, and the date.

Never collected: personal email addresses or phone numbers, home addresses, dates of birth, photos, nationality,
gender, family status, or any special category of data (Art. 9). No email address is ever derived from a naming
pattern, and no address is checked against a mail server.

## Sources

- Employer websites: home, team, about, contact and careers pages, read only where `robots.txt` allows it.
- Public job feeds that employers publish through their applicant tracking system (Recruitee, Lever, Greenhouse).
- The Belgian business register (KBO/BCE open data): company facts only, no people.
- Press releases and conference speaker pages that name a person together with their employer.
- The titles and excerpts that the Brave Search API returns for public professional profiles. LinkedIn pages
  themselves are never opened.

Pages behind a login are never read. CAPTCHAs are never bypassed. Every request carries a User-Agent that names the
tool and links to a page about it.

## Retention

Each contact is deleted automatically `DATA_RETENTION_DAYS` days after it was collected (180 by default). The check runs
when the API starts and every 24 hours while it runs. Spreadsheets generated by the tool state their deletion date on
their first sheet; deleting copies you saved elsewhere is up to you.

To delete data by hand:

```bash
pnpm data:purge -- --search <id>   # one search with its contacts, evidence and spreadsheets
pnpm data:purge -- --all           # everything collected
pnpm data:purge -- --expired       # run the retention rule now
```

`--all` keeps two things: the erasure blocklist (below), because deleting it would let erased people come back, and the
imported business register, which holds no people.

## Objection and erasure

Anyone can ask the controller to delete their data, and to stop collecting it (Art. 17 and 21). In the app, the
**Erase this contact** button on a contact's page:

1. deletes the contact and every piece of evidence about them;
2. deletes the spreadsheets of that search, since they may contain the person;
3. adds a blocklist entry (the normalised name and the employer's domain, nothing else), so that no later search adds
   them again.

People can also ask what data is held about them (Art. 15): the contact page shows all of it, with its sources.

They also have the right to complain to a data protection authority. In Belgium that is the Autorité de protection
des données / Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit (https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be).

## Informing the people you contact (Art. 14)

The people in the results never see this app, so a page inside it does not inform them. Art. 14 asks you to tell them,
at the latest in your first message. Add a short notice to that message, and host this file somewhere public (for
example the repository page) so you can link to it.
